Chess App · Custom Variant

♟️ Chess The Phoenix Core

A standard chess game featuring a custom mode called The Phoenix Core, where each player secretly carries a “Phoenix” piece that can rescue their King from checkmate once per game.

🔥 The Phoenix & Dice Rule

Starts on your King's square (E1 for White, E8 for Black). It cannot capture or block. Every 3 of your turns, normal moves are blocked until you roll the die and move the Phoenix (a free action that resets the 3-turn counter). It cannot land on your own pieces, and sliding pieces stop when they hit one.

👑 The Revival & Safety Rules

If checkmated, your King teleports to wherever your Phoenix is, the Phoenix disappears (once per game), and play continues. Revival fails if the Phoenix's square is attacked by an enemy piece, if it's still on the King's square, or if teleporting leaves you in check again.

Interactive Phoenix Die Simulator
Roll the die to see how your Phoenix moves on Turn 3!

Strategy: The game is a balance between playing normal chess and parking your Phoenix somewhere safe.

04 · Mind & Personality

How I Think, Learn & Stay Curious

🧠 How I Think

I think analytically and creatively, but also emotionally. I believe emotions are beautiful because they make experiences meaningful, although they can sometimes lead to impulsive decisions. That is why analytical thinking is important to me: it helps me understand situations more clearly and make better decisions.

I enjoy exploring ideas beyond the boundaries of a standard textbook — extending into dinosaurs, paleontology, space, galaxies, nebulae, black holes, dark matter, quantum mechanics, materials, engineering, and 3D visualization.

When I encounter something interesting, I don't always want to stop at understanding what it does. I want to understand how it works, why it works, and whether I can use that knowledge to create something of my own.

When I encounter something I don't understand, I try to approach it logically and analytically first. If that doesn't work, I approach it creatively, looking for different perspectives and ways of understanding the problem.

I prefer reasoning and creative problem-solving over physical experimentation, partly because school and time constraints limit what I can practically experiment with. However, I enjoy experimenting in other ways — many of my interests, including app development, chess, and cubing, began through exploration and experimentation.

I also have a competitive spirit. I enjoy improving, challenging myself, and seeing how far I can push my abilities.
